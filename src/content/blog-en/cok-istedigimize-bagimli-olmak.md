“Happiness lies not in obtaining what we desire, but in being free from what we do not desire.” — Epictetus

We often hear, “If you want something enough, you will achieve it.” A small but crucial addition helps: if you truly want something and can still stay grounded without it, you can approach it more evenly. Goals we depend on often narrow our freedom. Desire can be creative; dependency is desire that has turned into fear.

“I want a lot of money” differs from “I am terrified of having no money.” Wanting a relationship also differs from believing that being alone makes you less worthy. Without recognising this distinction, you may think you are pursuing a goal when you are actually escaping fear. Paradoxically, this can move you further from what you want, because decisions are driven by the fear of losing yourself.

In these situations, the coaching app helps you see the beliefs beneath your wishes. Working through questions rather than advice can clarify your emotional attachment. Instead of urging you to want more, it gives you room to understand what you are holding on to. As research often highlights, excessive fixation on outcomes can increase anxiety, while flexibility can support learning and action.

A healthy approach does not mean your goal stops mattering. It means approaching it more openly, calmly and creatively. Separating fear from desire can restore a sense of choice. Facing “What if it does not happen?” may feel difficult initially, but can also reduce the burden.

This is where the coaching app's approach becomes clear. It makes the fear behind dependency visible rather than simply encouraging you to want more. You do not have to abandon your goal; you can approach it from a clearer place. That clarity can support better decisions, clearer boundaries and more sustainable effort.

Once this awareness takes root, pursuing a goal can become less pressured and more sustainable. Moving from “This must happen” towards “This matters and is worth working for” can make effort flow more freely.

When desire becomes dependency, people often attach their worth to the outcome. Without a promotion, they will feel inadequate. Without a relationship, they will feel unlovable. Without earning money, they believe they cannot feel safe. When these connections remain hidden, a goal becomes a test of personal worth. Even a small delay can then feel like a major threat.

Questions in the coaching app clarify your relationship with the goal rather than making the goal smaller. “What would change in your life if this happened?”, “What would you conclude about yourself if it did not?” and “Which need is behind this wish?” can help reveal the underlying need. It might be security, recognition, freedom or belonging.

This distinction can be empowering. Once you recognise the need, you can begin to see that a single outcome is not your only route. If money represents security, there may be other small steps that also support feeling safe. If a relationship represents belonging, you can explore connection without relying entirely on one person's response. The goal can still matter without containing all of your worth.
