“When you say no, you are also saying yes to something that matters to you.” This captures an often misunderstood aspect of boundaries. Many people see saying no as harsh, rude or unloving. Yet setting a boundary means trying to keep a sense of yourself within a relationship, rather than ending it.

People who struggle to say no usually have good intentions. They care about those around them. To avoid upsetting anyone, leaving someone in difficulty or being misunderstood, they put their own needs last. At first, this behaviour may look accommodating. Over time, however, they grow tired, anger builds and they begin to resent the things they agreed to do.

The issue goes beyond being unable to say the word “no”. The deeper question is: what would saying no mean about me? Am I selfish? Will I be unloved? Would I stop being a good person? The coaching app helps bring these hidden beliefs into view. Changing a behaviour begins with recognising the fear it is tied to.

The same request may be a simple favour for one person and a burden carried for years for another. There is no single right boundary for everyone.

Sometimes a boundary begins with a short sentence: “I cannot do that today.” “This is not a good time for me.” “I need time to think about it.” These words may feel unfamiliar at first. You are sending a new message both to the other person and to your own established habit.

In a coaching session, you can explore the need behind the guilt. Perhaps you want acceptance, avoid conflict or associate being a good person with constant sacrifice. Recognising this can make setting a boundary feel more like an honest act and less like a harsh one.
